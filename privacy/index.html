Privacy Policy

Effective date: 13 November 2026

Spotter is a workout tracker with friends, squads and an AI coach. It is made by Uygar Acar ("we", "us"). You can reach us at uygar0605@icloud.com.

This policy says what Spotter collects, why, who else sees it, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. We wrote it to be read.

The short version

  • Spotter works without an account. Until you sign in, your workouts and everything else you log stay on your phone and are not sent to us.
  • Spotter sends anonymous usage data (which screens and features are used, with counts and yes or no answers, never your name, workouts, photos or health data) tied to a random number that is not your account. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • If you sign in, we store your workouts and the things you choose to share, so they follow you across phones and your friends can see what you share.
  • We do not run ads, we do not track you across other apps and websites, and we do not sell your data.
  • Apple Health data stays on your phone, with one exception you control: if you turn on "Share heart rate" for a post, that post carries your average and max heart rate and the name of the device that recorded it. We never use Health data for ads and never sell it.
  • If you use the AI coach, a summary of your training and your chat are sent to OpenAI to write the answer.
  • You can delete your account, and everything on it, inside the app.

What stays on your phone

Spotter is local-first. Your workouts, routines, custom exercises, settings and progress are saved on your phone first. If you never sign in, they never leave it. Apple Health works on your phone: Spotter can save your workouts to Health, read your body weight (to turn "2x bodyweight" into a number) and, once you allow it, read your heart rate over each workout (to show your average and highest). Your body weight and your saved workouts in Health are never uploaded, and a heart rate is not uploaded either unless you share it with a post (see "What you share"). Your heart rate is kept on your phone with the workout it belongs to. The photos you take for proof, and the photos you add to a workout (from the camera, or picked from your library, which needs no access to it), are kept on your phone until you share them with a post.

The answers you give while setting Spotter up stay on your phone even when you have an account: what usually gets in the way of training (which can include low energy or injuries), where you train, how many days you trained last week, and your streak goal. They are never uploaded, never shown to other people and never given to the coach. (The program builder starts its equipment question from where you train; the equipment you confirm there is one of the five answers it sends for a program.) The workout cards you make to share are drawn on your phone. Spotter adds one to your photo library only when you tap Save to Photos, and it cannot read your library. Sharing a card to Instagram Stories opens Instagram on your phone and gives it the picture; Spotter sends Meta nothing.

What we collect when you sign in

You sign in with Apple, with Google, or with an email address and a password you choose. With Apple or Google we have no password for you at all. A password you make for an email account is stored only as a salted hash by our sign-in provider: we cannot read it, and nobody at Spotter will ever ask you for it. We do not send email to confirm the address. So that you can set a new password if you forget it, a phone you sign in on keeps a recovery key in its keychain (your iCloud Keychain carries it to your other devices if you use it); our server keeps only a hash of it.

Anonymous usage data

To see which parts of Spotter people use and where they get stuck, Spotter sends us anonymous usage data. It works with or without an account, and it is on until you turn it off (Profile, Settings, Safety and support, Share anonymous usage data).

What is sent. The screens you open and the features you use, as short events: for example that a workout was saved, with how many exercises and sets it had, which length band it falls in (such as 45 to 60 minutes), whether it had a photo or heart rate, and who it was shared with (everyone, followers or only you), or that the paywall was shown and which plan was chosen. Never what you wrote or logged: no names, handles, bios, captions, notes, messages, workout titles, exercises, weights or reps, photos, or heart rate values, and no ids of people, posts or squads. With the events go a few facts about the install: the app version, iOS version, device model (such as iPhone), your language and region, your units, your goal and experience level, your weekly workout target, and whether you have Premium, an account or a squad.

How it is tied together. Spotter makes a random number the first time it needs one and keeps it on your phone. It is not your account, your Apple ID, your advertising identifier or any identifier of your phone's maker, and we do not link it to your account or to anything else we hold about you. Turning the switch off deletes anything not yet sent and forgets the number; if you turn it on again you start as someone new. Because the number is random and unlinked, we cannot later find the data that came from you, so turning the switch off is how you stop it.

Who handles it. Phase (phase.sh), a privacy-focused analytics service, receives it for us as our service provider. Like any server, it sees the network address your phone connects from; Spotter tells it not to work out a location. We use the data only to understand and improve Spotter. We do not use it for advertising, we do not combine it with data from other companies' apps or websites, we do not use it to track you across apps, and we do not sell it. We keep it only as long as it is useful for that.

How we use it

To run Spotter: sign you in, sync your data, show your friends what you chose to share, score challenges and squad weeks, send the notifications you asked for, answer you from the coach, and check your subscription. To keep it safe: review reports, remove content that breaks the rules, and close accounts that abuse it. To answer you when you write to us. We do not use your data for advertising and we do not build advertising profiles.

Who else sees it

Other people, as you choose. Your name, handle, bio, home gym, level and workout count are visible to people who are signed in. A post is visible to everyone, to your followers, or only to you, as you choose each time. If you shared your heart rate with a post, everyone who can see the post can see it. Likes and comments are visible to people who can see the post. A nudge goes to one person. The people you follow, who follow you, or share a squad with can see how many training days you have done this week (a count, not the workouts). Squadmates see the squad's streak, callouts and forfeits. If you block someone, neither of you sees the other any more.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Reports, blocking and moderation

You can report any post, comment, person, message, squad or challenge. Reports are private: the person reported is not told who reported them. A person on our team reads reports, aims to act on them within 24 hours, removes content that breaks our rules and can close accounts. You can block anyone; they are not told. We keep reports so the people they protect stay protected. When an account is deleted, the reports it sent stay but no longer name it, and reports about it stay too. We delete a report 12 months after it is closed.

Apple Health

Spotter asks permission before it uses Health. It can write your workouts to Health, read your body weight and read your heart rate over a workout (from an Apple Watch or another device that writes to Health). Your body weight and your workouts in Health stay on your phone: we do not upload them.

Your heart rate is shown to you and kept on your phone with the workout. It leaves your phone only when you turn on "Share heart rate" for a post, which is off until you turn it on once and is your choice for each post. Then the post carries your average and max heart rate and the device's name, and the people who can see the post can see them. If you delete the post, make it private, or choose "Remove heart rate" in its menu, they are removed from our server too. Heart rate is not part of what syncs between your phones and is never given to the AI coach.

We never use Health data for advertising or marketing, we do not sell it, we do not give it to data brokers, and Spotter does not use iCloud storage for it (your phone's own backup, if you have one, includes the app's data as it does for any app). You can turn heart rate off in Spotter (Profile, Settings, Apple Health) or in the Health app.

Push notifications

When you allow notifications and sign in, your phone's push token is stored with your account so our server can send you nudges, hype, squad alerts and challenge updates through Apple. You choose which kinds you want and quiet hours in Profile, Settings, Notifications. The token is removed when you sign out or delete your account, and Apple tells us when a token stops working. Delivery history is deleted after two weeks.

How long we keep it

  • Your account's data: until you delete the account. Deleting it is immediate and permanent: your profile, workouts, routines, posts, photos, comments, likes, follows, nudges, squads membership, challenge entries and settings are removed, and, if you signed in with Apple, the Sign in with Apple link is revoked. A squad or challenge you started stays for the people who are still in it, with its name and rules but without your name on it.
  • Backups: our provider keeps backups for up to 30 days; deleted data leaves them when they expire.
  • Reports: see above.
  • Notification history: two weeks.
  • Coach: we keep your Premium record and a count of daily requests as long as your account exists. We do not keep your chat on our server; it lives on your phone. OpenAI's retention is covered above.
  • Anonymous usage data: with Phase, for as long as it is useful for understanding and improving Spotter. Anything on your phone that has not been sent yet is deleted when you turn the switch off.
  • Logs: our functions' logs (counts and error codes only) and our provider's request logs, for the short period our provider keeps them.

Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account: Profile, Settings, Account, Delete account.
  • Edit your profile: Profile, Edit profile (name, handle, bio).
  • Choose who sees a post: on the Save workout screen, per workout. Share heart rate is a switch there, per post, and "Remove heart rate" in the post's menu takes it off again.
  • Turn off anonymous usage data: Profile, Settings, Safety and support, Share anonymous usage data. It is on until you turn it off.
  • Turn off notifications, Health, or the account's sync: in Spotter's Settings, or in iOS Settings.
  • Ask for a copy, a correction, or to object to or restrict how we use your data: write to uygar0605@icloud.com. We reply within 30 days.
  • If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland: you have the rights of access, correction, deletion, portability, restriction and objection under the GDPR, and the right to complain to your data protection authority. We process your data to provide the service you asked for (contract), to keep it safe (legitimate interest), and where you allow it (consent, which you can withdraw).
  • If you are in California or another US state with a privacy law: you can ask what we hold, to correct or delete it. We do not sell or share personal information for advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of.

Children

Spotter is not for children under 13 (or the age of digital consent where you live). We do not knowingly collect data from them. If you think a child has an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Where your data goes

Our providers are in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany), in the United States (OpenAI, Apple, Google) and, for anonymous usage data, wherever Phase runs its service. When data crosses a border we rely on the safeguards the law allows, such as standard contractual clauses.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections. Each person can read and change only their own private data: the database enforces it, not only the app. Our secret keys never ship in the app. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach that affects you we will tell you as the law requires.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will tell you in the app before it applies. The date at the top changes whenever the text does.
